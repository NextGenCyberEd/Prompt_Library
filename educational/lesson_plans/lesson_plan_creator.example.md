# 📝 Rhetorical Analysis of Persuasive Speeches - Lesson Plan

## 📋 Lesson Overview

**Subject:** English Language Arts  
**Grade Level:** 11th Grade  
**Topic:** Rhetorical Analysis of Persuasive Speeches  
**Duration:** 90-minute block period  
**Unit Context:** Part of a larger persuasive writing and rhetoric unit

**Curriculum Standards:**  
- CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RI.11-12.6: Determine an author's point of view and analyze how rhetoric is used to advance that point of view
- CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RI.11-12.3: Analyze a complex set of ideas or sequence of events and explain how specific individuals, ideas, or events interact and develop
- CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.SL.11-12.3: Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric

**Essential Questions:**
- How do speakers use language to persuade their audience?
- What makes rhetorical strategies effective in different contexts?
- How can we evaluate the effectiveness of persuasive techniques?

**Enduring Understandings:**
- Speakers deliberately choose language and rhetorical devices to influence audiences
- The effectiveness of rhetorical strategies depends on audience, context, and purpose
- Analyzing rhetoric helps us become more critical consumers of information

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

**Content Objectives:**
- Students will identify at least five rhetorical devices used in famous speeches
- Students will explain how specific rhetorical choices advance a speaker's purpose
- Students will evaluate the effectiveness of rhetorical strategies for specific audiences

**Skill Objectives:**
- Students will analyze written and spoken text for rhetorical devices
- Students will annotate speeches to identify ethos, pathos, and logos appeals
- Students will construct short written responses analyzing rhetorical effectiveness

**Language Objectives:**
- Students will use academic vocabulary related to rhetoric and persuasion
- Students will articulate rhetorical analysis using complete sentences and academic language
- ELL students will identify and define key rhetorical terms with examples

**Higher-Order Thinking Goals:**
- Students will evaluate how context influences rhetorical choices
- Students will compare effectiveness of different rhetorical strategies across speeches
- Students will defend judgments about rhetorical effectiveness with textual evidence

**Social-Emotional Connections:**
- Students will practice respectful discussion of potentially divisive speech topics
- Students will reflect on how language can influence emotions and perspectives

## 🧠 Prior Knowledge & Misconceptions

**Required Foundational Knowledge:**
- Basic understanding of persuasive techniques
- Familiarity with ethos, pathos, and logos appeals
- Understanding of audience and purpose in writing

**Common Student Misconceptions:**
- Confusing rhetorical questions with other question types
- Believing that emotional appeals (pathos) are always manipulative
- Assuming that effective rhetoric must be complex or elaborate
- Thinking that rhetorical analysis only applies to historical speeches

**Pre-Assessment Strategy:**
- Quick-write: "What makes a speech persuasive?" (collected and reviewed)
- Terminology matching warm-up activity
- Small group discussion of a recent persuasive message they've encountered

**Activation Techniques:**
- Think-pair-share about a time they were persuaded to change their mind
- K-W-L chart on rhetorical devices
- Video clip of a contemporary persuasive advertisement

**Connections to Previous Lessons:**
- Builds on previous lessons about persuasive writing techniques
- Extends understanding of author's purpose and tone

## ⏱️ Lesson Sequence

### **Warm-up/Do Now (10 min)**
- Students enter to find the prompt on board: "Think of a time someone convinced you to change your mind or take an action. What techniques did they use?" (3 min writing)
- Think-pair-share discussion with an elbow partner (2 min)
- Whole class sharing of effective persuasive techniques (3 min)
- Teacher introduces lesson objectives and essential question: "How do great speakers use language to persuade their audience?" (2 min)

### **Direct Instruction (15 min)**
- PowerPoint presentation reviewing rhetorical triangle (ethos, pathos, logos) with contemporary examples (5 min)
- Introduction to key rhetorical devices with examples:
  - Repetition (anaphora, epistrophe)
  - Parallelism
  - Metaphor and imagery
  - Antithesis
  - Rhetorical questions
  - Allusion
- Model annotation of a short speech excerpt on projector, thinking aloud to demonstrate identification of devices and analysis of effectiveness (5 min)
- Check for understanding: Quick "thumbs up/middle/down" self-assessment of comfort with identifying each device (2 min)

### **Guided Practice (25 min)**
- Distribute speech packet with three short speech excerpts of varying complexity
- Whole class works together on first excerpt (MLK's "I Have a Dream") (10 min)
  - Teacher guides annotation process using colored highlighters
  - Ethos - blue, Pathos - pink, Logos - green
  - Circle and label specific rhetorical devices
- Small group collaborative work on second excerpt (JFK's Inaugural Address) (10 min)
  - Groups of 3-4 students with mixed ability levels
  - Teacher circulates to provide feedback and support
  - ELL students receive speech with pre-highlighted key phrases
- Quick gallery walk of group annotations (5 min)
  - Each group posts their annotated speech
  - Students do a quick walk to see other groups' work
  - Optional: Add sticky notes with comments or questions

### **Independent Practice (25 min)**
- Individual analysis of third speech excerpt (Reagan's Challenger Disaster Address or Obama's "A More Perfect Union")
  - Differentiated options:
    - Standard: Full speech with guided questions
    - Support: Shorter excerpt with simplified language notes
    - Challenge: Compare two speeches with minimal guidance
  - Students complete rhetorical analysis graphic organizer (15 min)
  - Write a short paragraph evaluating the most effective rhetorical strategy in the speech and explaining why (10 min)

### **Closure (15 min)**
- Small group discussion: "What makes rhetorical strategies effective in different situations?" (5 min)
- Whole class share-out of key insights (5 min)
- Exit ticket: "Identify one rhetorical device from today's lesson that you could use in your own persuasive writing. Explain how and why you would use it." (5 min)

## 📊 Assessment Strategies

**Formative Assessment:**
- Observation of participation during guided practice
- Quality of speech annotations (collected)
- Responses to discussion questions
- Exit ticket responses
- Graphic organizer completion

**Summative Assessment Options:**
- Rhetorical analysis paragraph (can be expanded into a full essay)
- Speech comparison chart with evaluation of relative effectiveness
- Rhetorical device identification quiz (follow-up lesson)
- Original persuasive paragraph using at least three rhetorical devices

**Performance Indicators:**
- Accurate identification of rhetorical devices with text evidence
- Clear explanation of how devices advance the author's purpose
- Thoughtful evaluation of effectiveness with specific reasoning
- Appropriate use of academic vocabulary in discussions and writing

**Rubric Elements:**
- Identification: Can identify rhetorical devices with textual evidence
- Analysis: Can explain how devices create specific effects
- Evaluation: Can judge effectiveness with clear reasoning
- Application: Can apply rhetorical concepts in new contexts

**Self-Assessment Opportunity:**
- Rhetorical device confidence checklist (students rate their understanding of each device)
- Exit ticket reflection on their own learning

## 🔄 Differentiation Strategies

**For Advanced Learners:**
- Comparative analysis of two speeches on similar topics
- Analysis of more complex or nuanced rhetorical strategies (e.g., concession)
- Opportunity to find and analyze a speech of their choosing
- Creation of original persuasive paragraph using multiple devices

**For Struggling Students:**
- Simplified speech excerpts with fewer rhetorical devices
- Pre-highlighted examples of devices in speeches
- Sentence starters for analysis responses
- Graphic organizers with more structure
- Option to work with partner for annotation

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual reference sheet for rhetorical devices
- Speeches with glossary of challenging vocabulary
- Option to use native language for initial analysis notes
- Partner support from peer with same language background when possible
- Audio recordings of speeches to support comprehension

**For Students with IEPs:**
- Reading support accommodations as specified
- Extended time for written responses
- Option for oral response instead of written
- Pre-highlighted text features
- Chunked text with guided questions

**For Different Learning Styles:**
- Visual: Color-coding, graphic organizers, YouTube clips
- Auditory: Speech recordings, discussion, think-aloud modeling
- Kinesthetic: Gallery walk, sorting activity, physical movement to group by device type
- Reading/Writing: Annotation, written analysis, reference sheets

- Jigsaw approach with expert groups for each rhetorical device
