# Feedback on Persuasive Essay: School Start Times

Dear Emma,

## 👏 Strengths

Your passion for the topic of later school start times truly shines through in this essay. You've done impressive work incorporating scientific research to support your position—the sleep studies from Stanford and the American Academy of Pediatrics were particularly well-chosen and effectively integrated. Your conclusion stands out as a highlight, with its compelling call to action that leaves the reader motivated to consider change. I especially appreciated how you connected the issue to our own school community, making the argument relevant and immediate for your audience.

## 🎯 Areas for Growth

While your argument has strong elements, there are a few areas that could elevate this essay to the next level:

**Thesis Development:** Your current thesis statement ("School should start later because it's better for students") captures your position but lacks specificity about the key arguments you'll make. A more precise thesis would provide a roadmap for your reader and strengthen your overall persuasive impact.

**Organizational Structure:** The flow between your arguments sometimes feels disconnected, particularly in the transition between academic benefits and mental health considerations. A more deliberate organization would help your reader follow your reasoning more easily.

**Consistency in Tone:** There are several moments where your writing shifts from formal academic language to a more conversational tone (e.g., "This is totally unfair" and "It's crazy to think that..."). While your authentic voice is valuable, maintaining a consistent formal tone throughout would strengthen your credibility.

## 🛠️ Actionable Guidance

To address these growth areas, consider these specific strategies:

**For your thesis:** Try revising your thesis to include your main supporting points, such as: "Schools should implement later start times because doing so would improve students' academic performance, support adolescent physical health needs, and reduce mental health issues among teenagers." This provides clearer direction for both you and your reader.

**For organization:** Consider creating a more explicit structure with clear topic sentences that connect directly to your thesis points. Try mapping your essay before revision, ensuring each paragraph has a clear focus that advances your overall argument.

**For tone consistency:** During revision, highlight phrases that sound conversational or informal. Replace emotional intensifiers ("totally," "really," "so") with more precise language. For example, instead of "This is totally unfair," try "This scheduling practice disadvantages adolescents whose biological needs differ from younger students."
